# Section 1

Working in tech with pain

I spent more than 20 years working in the tech industry, and for most of them, my body hurt. Neck, wrists, shoulders, lower back. I did everything the internet told me to do: standing desks, ergonomic keyboards, wrist guards, ergonomic mice, a high-end Herman Miller chair. Each fix helped for a while. Then the pain would eventually come back. I felt physically good when I was on vacation, but I thought who doesn't.

I just assumed this was part of the sacrifice to work in the tech industry.

## Section 2

Cycling through the pain

The pain followed me from the desk. In Boulder, Colorado I trained as an amateur racing cyclist and dealt with pain in my knees, back, shoulder, and neck. I saw chiropractors and physical therapists for hip issues but of course I kept riding all competitive cyclists know suffering is part of it. I even bought a lot of fitness related products to help my cause: CBD oils, supplements, foam rollers, ice packs, massagers, and more.

When I moved into CrossFit and weightlifting, the pattern continued — but the pain just showed up differently. Shoulders and back flared up on different lifts. I wore braces on my knees and elbows to take tension off the joints. It seemed like I was always managing some new pain and every workout felt like I was the kid from A Christmas Story getting ready to go to school.

For a long time For a long time I just thought this was the price for staying fit and active.

## Section 3

The year that broke me

Then came 2026. Everything stacked up at once. A minor injury from a home improvement project turned into a debilitating frozen shoulder. The injury was very small but what made it worse was a nervous system already carrying more pressure than it could handle. My shoulder froze because my system was overloaded. My body telling me something had to change immediately and it couldn't go on like this.

When I started learning about Pain reprocessing therapy (PRT) the pattern finally became clear. Looking back, the neck, the back, the knees, the shoulders: nearly all of it traced to the same root. Chronic stress and a nervous system I hadn't learned to regulate. I'd been treating symptoms while the source kept running in the background.

I was fixated on every moment of pain and minor discomfort, and that fixation kept me locked in a chronic pain-fear cycle. I could push through it when I was younger. Now that I'm in my later forties, my body won't let me and I'm so grateful it finally took a stand.

That realization is why I do this work. Pain reprocessing therapy (PRT) and somatic coaching gave me a different way to understand pain — a way to work with my nervous system instead of bypassing what it was trying to tell me. Yes, ergonomics, diet, form, good equipment and rest all matter. But for me, lasting change started when I stopped asking "what's wrong with my body?" and started asking:

- "What is my nervous system's capacity right now?"
- "What is underneath this pain?"
- "How can I engage with the pain so I'm not consumed by it?"
- "How can I handle daily life stressors better?"
- "What if I stopped pushing through everything and actually listened to my body?"

If this story resonates with you, let's talk. Pain is part of life — you can't avoid it. But you can learn to engage with it so it doesn't become the loudest signal in your body. PRT helped me, and that's why I created Excelsior LLC.

### Section 4

What does Excelsior mean?

Excelsior is a Latin word meaning "higher," "loftier," or "ever upward." Stan Lee, the creator of Marvel Comics, said it often. He was an inspiration to me. I want to help people get to a higher place in their own lives and get them feeling better.
